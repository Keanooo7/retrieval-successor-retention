## Q11: checking the new claim from the formulas

The claim (`:396`) is that under FIFO the two conventions agree for sentences censored at the
stream end and differ by 1 for evicted sentences.

Take a FIFO memory of `M` slots, where sentence `i` is written at step `i` and the stream has `S`
sentences. Sentence `i` is evicted at step `e = i + M` if `i + M ≤ S − 1`. Otherwise it is censored.

| | Evicted (`i ≤ S − M − 1`) | Censored (`i ≥ S − M`) |
|---|---|---|
| (A) `k = 1 + (e − i)` (D7; censored `1 + (S − 1 − i)`) | `M + 1` | `S − i` |
| (B) `min(M, S − i)` (correction 25) | `M` | `S − i` |
| A − B | **1** | **0** |

**The claim holds.** At the boundary, `i = S − M` gives A = B = `M`, and `i = S − M − 1` gives
A = `M + 1` against B = `M`. On synthetic data (`S = 48`, `M = 16`), sentences 0–31 score 17 under A
and 16 under B. Sentence 32 scores 16 under both, so under B it joins the evicted tie and under A
it sits alone just below that tie.

## Findings

### MAJOR-1: Q11's option (B) is misdescribed and, as written, defined only for FIFO (`:396`, `:224-225`)

1. **"Counting carry-overs only" is false for censored sentences.** D7 defines carry-overs as the
   forwards of steps `i+1 … e` (`:220-221`). For a censored sentence that is `S − 1 − i`, but B
   gives `S − i`. B equals the carry-over count for evicted sentences and exceeds it by one for
   censored ones. B is really the convention "survival = `min(e, S) − i`", which censors at `S`
   where A's count censors at `S − 1`.
2. **B is a closed form for FIFO only.** Correction 25 gives `min(M, S−i)` as FIFO's own
   deterministic survival, which is why the partial correlation (2) separates RSR from FIFO. E7's
   numbers (1) and (3) need survival under RSR and under the leading-edge arm, where `e` is not
   `i + M`. The row gives no general form for B, so Brendan would be picking between one complete
   convention and a formula that exists only for FIFO. State B's general form, for example
   `min(e, S) − i` with the censoring point named, or say that B has none.
3. **"Ties … can therefore reorder ranks" overstates the effect.** Under any policy, A − B is 1 for
   evicted sentences and 0 for censored ones. So any value difference between an evicted sentence
   and a censored one shrinks by exactly 1 from A to B. A gap of 1 becomes a tie, and a tie becomes
   a gap of −1. Gaps of 2 or more keep their sign. A strict order can therefore reverse only between
   an evicted and a censored sentence that were already tied or adjacent, and only by one step. The
   ranks within the evicted group and within the censored group are unchanged. Under FIFO the only
   effect is that the single sentence `i = S − M` joins the evicted tie under B. Say "can merge or
   split ties at the evicted/censored boundary". The row should still say this is not a constant
   offset, because it is not.
4. **D7 contradicts Q11.** `:224-225` still says the two conventions match "up to the off-by-one of
   where 'survival' starts". That implies the constant offset Q11 now correctly denies. Reword it to
   point to Q11's statement.
