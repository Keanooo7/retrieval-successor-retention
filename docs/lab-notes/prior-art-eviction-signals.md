# Prior art: attention-score KV-cache eviction and its critiques (phase-1 item 1f)

Written 2026-10-19. This is a literature note only. It makes no claim about RSR's
results and does not recommend anything for RSR.

**What this note checks.** Internal "finding 1" is UNVERIFIED and is being re-tested
separately. It says attention-derived retrieval scores rank the *top* of a memory well
but *misrank the bottom*, which is the end eviction acts on. The question here is
whether the KV-cache-eviction literature already reports this.

**How to read the citations.** Every quoted passage is verbatim from a source saved in
`~/research-corpus/sources/` (corpus commit `3779e54`). The citation form is
`[series/file.md:a-b]`, where `a-b` are line numbers in that saved file. The saved
files are pypdf text extractions of the arXiv PDFs, fetched 2026-10-19. Extraction
drops some inter-word spaces, so a few quotes contain run-together words exactly as
the saved text does. Each quote was machine-checked against its cited lines before
commit (whitespace-normalised, the same rule as `corpus.py validate`). Corpus cards
for each source live in `~/research-corpus/components/` and are named in §(e).

The note uses three tags:

- **shows**: the paper ran an experiment that measures the thing.
- **argues**: the paper reasons for the claim or offers it as motivation, with no
  direct measurement of that claim.
- **assumes**: the paper takes the claim as a premise.

## (a) Q1: is "attention-score eviction misranks the low end" known?

**Verdict: SHOWN in its weaker form, by a few small-sample 2026 preprints. The explicit
asymmetry (top ranked well, bottom misranked) has NOT been found as a direct
measurement for attention-score eviction.**

The weaker form is that low attention does not reliably identify entries that are safe
to drop. Four sources show it.

**1. AvoKV-E (Yu et al. 2026, arXiv:2610.03007v1).** This is the most direct evidence
found. The authors run a leave-one-out replay. They "replay the next Wfut=8 decode steps
twice from the same context, once with the full cache and once with candidate i’s KV
entry removed, and measure the average KL between the two next-token distributions,"
[kv-eviction-signals/yu-2026-avokv-e-payload-aware-eviction.md:785-787].

- **Main result.** "the blindspot cell {Nt(p i)≤0.2, Nt(bi)≥0.8} contains 10/43=23%
  high-damage states (Wilson 95% CI [13%, 38%]), against 81/844=9.6% over the remaining
  22 cells. The enrichment is 2.4× and one-sided Fisher’s exact gives p=0.009."
  [kv-eviction-signals/yu-2026-avokv-e-payload-aware-eviction.md:795-797]. Here
  attention percentile is at most 0.2 and value-norm percentile is at least 0.8.
- **Positive control.** The high-attention cells act as a check, and "confirm that the
  analysis recovers the obvious effect (high-attention tokens are damaging to remove)"
  [kv-eviction-signals/yu-2026-avokv-e-payload-aware-eviction.md:802-802].
- **Timing.** The authors also find "the worst-case lag is concentrated on exactly the
  low-attention candidates the policy is most eager to discard."
  [kv-eviction-signals/yu-2026-avokv-e-payload-aware-eviction.md:818-819].

This is the closest thing found to "right at the top, wrong at the bottom." It has
real limits:

- The pool is small: "We pool n=892 candidate KV entries from five MATH/AIME traces
  generated by DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B."
  [kv-eviction-signals/yu-2026-avokv-e-payload-aware-eviction.md:775-776].
- The blindspot cell holds only 43 entries.
- The same appendix states "The marginal distributions along each axis are individually
  flat, so neither attention nor value-norm alone discriminates damaging from safe
  candidates; both features are needed."
  [kv-eviction-signals/yu-2026-avokv-e-payload-aware-eviction.md:804-805].
- On a different pool of 5,440 candidates from 30 DeepSeek-R1 AIME traces, the
  attention-only row of Table 3 reads "attention only 0.891 0.369 0.621 0.033 0.322"
  [kv-eviction-signals/yu-2026-avokv-e-payload-aware-eviction.md:478-478]. The columns
  are AUROC, AP, top-k recall, false-safe rate and Spearman. An AUROC of 0.891 does not
  look like a flat marginal. The paper does not reconcile the two analyses; they use
  different pools and different labels.

**2. TwinKV v1 (Chen et al. 2026, arXiv:2608.27128v1).** The paper ran a leave-one-out
probe on context chunks. It reports "pooled Spearmanρ=−0.004(p= 0.96), computed overn=
136chunks. Chunks that received essentially no attention span the full range of true
marginal utility, including some of the most useful chunks in the entire pool"
[kv-eviction-signals/chen-2026-twinkv-v1-leave-one-out-probe.md:157-160].

Caveats:

- The sample is 136 chunks.
- The probe passage does not name the model.
- The abstract says "only weakly related" while the figure title says "Attention tracks
  nothing about true causal utility"
  [kv-eviction-signals/chen-2026-twinkv-v1-leave-one-out-probe.md:225-225].
- The figure label reads "Spearman = 0.004" while the text says −0.004
  [kv-eviction-signals/chen-2026-twinkv-v1-leave-one-out-probe.md:221-222].
- **The probe is absent from v4 of the same arXiv id**
  [kv-eviction-signals/chen-2026-twinkv-v4-frequency-corrections.md]. Version 4 was
  retitled and reframed. That absence is my observation from reading v4; the paper
  gives no reason for dropping the probe. Treat this as a withdrawn or superseded
  result.

**3. QEvict (Garg et al. 2026, arXiv:2608.05326v1).** The paper ran a diagnostic on one
model with a 20% budget. Under token-level cumulative-attention eviction, Future Missed
Mass, which is the share of the next 32 steps' full-cache attention that lands on
already-discarded states, is "= 1 51.73% 0.0170"
[kv-eviction-signals/garg-2026-qevict-recoverable-eviction.md:1191-1191]. That is 51.73%
in the R1 row. The paper also reports "The low oracle LIR in Table 16 confirms that the
true top-ranked region is largely persistent."
[kv-eviction-signals/garg-2026-qevict-recoverable-eviction.md:1267-1267]. Approximate
scoring re-admits about 6.3 times as many windows as the oracle
[kv-eviction-signals/garg-2026-qevict-recoverable-eviction.md:1267-1269]. The target
here is future *attention*, not output damage.

**4. Random Attention (Wang et al. 2026, arXiv:2609.03430v2).** The paper ran experiments
on reasoning models. Once the prompt is protected, the scored evictors (SnapKV, R-KV,
VaSE) do no better than a policy that evicts at random: "with the prompt protected,
each of the three still trails a policy that ranks nothing"
[kv-eviction-signals/wang-2026-random-attention.md:342-343]. So within the reasoning
trace, the score's choice of what to drop beats nothing. The exception is a fact stated
once and needed much later. Attention-window scores then almost never keep it: the row
reads "SnapKV0.004−11.11" [kv-eviction-signals/wang-2026-random-attention.md:444-444].

**Learned scorers, not attention-score eviction.**

- **KVP (Moschella et al. 2026)** shows the same top-versus-tail asymmetry, but for a
  *learned regressor* trained on future attention: "Regression’s red/orange bars
  concentrate on theright tail— the many unimportant tokens. This is the classical
  overfitting signature of magnitude-sensitive losses on heavy-tailed targets: most of
  the loss is paid by the left-tail outliers, so the optimizer leaves the right-tail
  ordering approximately random."
  [kv-eviction-signals/moschella-2026-kvp-learning-to-evict.md:993-995]. This is shown
  for a single head ("Figure 9b zooms in on a single head (L10.H0)."
  [kv-eviction-signals/moschella-2026-kvp-learning-to-evict.md:990-990]). It concerns a
  learned scorer's loss, not H2O-style attention scores.
- **Steele (2026)** is weaker, null-style evidence. On TinyLlama, H2O and random
  selection are within each other's confidence intervals at every retention level
  (rows at [kv-eviction-signals/steele-2026-limits-of-learned-importance-scoring.md:199-202]).

**Counter-evidence: attention ranks well when the queries are right.**

- **LORE-KV.** With future-like queries held fixed, swapping the deletion-cost objective
  for accumulated attention mass barely matters: "B= 128, changing the deletion
  objective changes the LongBench average by at most0.09points, indicating that the
  query reference has a substantially larger effect than the exact deletion criterion."
  [kv-eviction-signals/bilal-2026-lore-kv-monte-carlo-eviction.md:1568-1569].
- **Scissorhands and SnapKV** show that high attention persists, but they measure
  attention against later attention (§b).

**What was not found.** No paper reports ranking quality of an attention score
*stratified by score region*, meaning top quantile versus bottom quantile measured
against an output-damage oracle. AvoKV-E's attention-by-value-norm grid is the nearest
thing.

## (c) Other critiques

**Attention sinks: high attention without semantic importance (top end).**

- StreamingLLM *shows* that initial tokens collect attention "irrespective of their
  relevance to the language modeling task"
  [kv-eviction-signals/xiao-2024-streamingllm-attention-sinks.md:112-113].
- It *shows* that "the absolute position of the starting tokens, rather than their
  semantic value, holds greater significance."
  [kv-eviction-signals/xiao-2024-streamingllm-attention-sinks.md:225-227].
- So attention magnitude and semantic value come apart at the top end. Removing a sink
  is still catastrophic, because sinks hold the softmax denominator
  [kv-eviction-signals/xiao-2024-streamingllm-attention-sinks.md:214-215].

**Recency bias in accumulated scores.**

- RoCo *argues* that recent tokens' "recorded importance scores for some attention-
  based methods can be underestimated and thus get wrongly evicted."
  [kv-eviction-signals/ren-2024-roco-efficacy-of-eviction-policy.md:316-319].
- It *shows* that, measured against full-cache attention, "MAS has remarkably higher
  consistency among all methods, achieving over 0.9 Jaccard sim- ilarity even at 0.3
  cache budget rate." [kv-eviction-signals/ren-2024-roco-efficacy-of-eviction-policy.md:383-385].
- This compares attention with attention, not with output damage.

**Intermittent attention and premature eviction.**

- G-KV *shows* that top sets drift across observation windows, and *argues* that "some
  tokens that possess long-term importance are likely to be prematurely evicted due to
  being temporar- ily overlooked by a single window."
  [kv-eviction-signals/liao-2025-g-kv-global-attention.md:165-168].
- The same paper *shows* that overlap with the union of earlier windows "approaches
  95%" [kv-eviction-signals/liao-2025-g-kv-global-attention.md:172-173].

**Small evicted attention mass, large output error.**

- MomentKV *shows*, on LLaMA-3-8B with H2O selection, that "The evicted mass wE is
  small, averaging below 0.08 at L=128"
  [kv-eviction-signals/li-2026-momentkv-directional-gap.md:287-288].
- Yet "The near-orthogonality amplifies residual mass into over 20% relative error on
  average, confirming that the directional gap, not the evicted mass, is the dominant
  error source." [kv-eviction-signals/li-2026-momentkv-directional-gap.md:290-292].
- This says the attention mass on evicted entries understates the damage of evicting
  them.

**Attention mass versus answer relevance on structured input.**

- This comes from Mandal 2026, already in the corpus as
  `memory-retention/mandal-2026-structural-role-bias-kv-eviction.md`.
- It *shows* that "structural KEY tokens are over-retained at roughly1.8×the rate of the
  answer-carrying V ALUE tokens, collapsing exact-match accuracy from 88% to 0% at a 5%
  budget" [memory-retention/mandal-2026-structural-role-bias-kv-eviction.md:17-20].
- This is again a top-end failure: the wrong tokens get high mass.

**Heuristics as proxies for future utility.**

- KVP *argues* that recency and past attention "serve only as indirect proxies for a
  token’s future utility"
  [kv-eviction-signals/moschella-2026-kvp-learning-to-evict.md:8-9].
- It *shows* that "Importance estimated by the fixed sink-and-recency heuristic
  ofStreamingLLM deviates substantially from true importance ranking."
  [kv-eviction-signals/moschella-2026-kvp-learning-to-evict.md:70-71].
- Its own ground truth is future attention, which it calls "a proxy for task-level
  utility" [kv-eviction-signals/moschella-2026-kvp-learning-to-evict.md:662-663].

**Knockout-based versus attention-based importance.**

- TwinKV v1's leave-one-out probe (§a, later withdrawn) and AvoKV-E's leave-one-out KL
  oracle (§a) are knockout-based.
- LORE-KV scores by "projected leave-one-out attention-output deletion cost"
  [kv-eviction-signals/bilal-2026-lore-kv-monte-carlo-eviction.md:21-23]. It finds the
  choice between that and accumulated attention mass matters little once the query
  reference is fixed (§a).

## (d) Not found / gaps

**Searched for and not found:**

- A measurement of attention-score ranking quality *stratified by score region* (bottom
  quantile versus top quantile) against an output-damage oracle. Nothing reports, for
  example, precision@bottom-k against Spearman at the top.
- In H2O, Scissorhands, TOVA or SnapKV, any ablation showing that the tokens they evicted
  (lowest-scored) were later needed. Their checks are top-removal (H2O), attention
  against later attention (Scissorhands persistence ratio, SnapKV hit rate), or
  end-task accuracy at a budget.
- A persistence-of-importance test whose target is output damage rather than later
  attention. QEvict's revival rate and G-KV's window overlap also use attention as the
  target.

**Seen only in search-engine summaries, not saved, not read, therefore not cited
above:**

- TRIM-KV (ICLR 2026), the token-retention paper behind the
  `proceedings.iclr.cc/.../c3887741...` URL.
- ValueDiff (arXiv:2609.23314; a summary claimed "top-256 Jaccard ≈0.4" for attention
  rankings).
- HashEvict (arXiv:2412.16187).
- Expected Attention (arXiv:2510.00636).
- PagedEviction (arXiv:2509.04377).
- The NVIDIA KV-compression blog.

**Not searched for in depth:** Keyformer, Quest, InfiniGen, FastGen, PyramidKV, Ada-KV,
KeyDiff, R-KV, VaSE, TriAttention. Random Attention [kv-eviction-signals/wang-2026-random-attention.md]
characterises the last four only in its own notation.

**Peer-review status was not checked for any source.** Papers with 2026 arXiv ids are
preprints as far as their own front matter shows.

**What the strongest Q1 evidence rests on:**
