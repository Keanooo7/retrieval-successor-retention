## 1. What the project is

**Thought Gestalt (TG)** — [P2], Borazjanizadeh & McClelland (Stanford), arXiv:2512.25026v2, under
review — is a recurrent transformer that reads one sentence per step, compresses each sentence into a
single vector ("gestalt"), and keeps a fixed memory of `M` slots. When memory is full it evicts
**FIFO**. Nobody chose FIFO on purpose; it is a default.

**RSR (Retrieval-Successor Retention)** replaces that FIFO eviction with a *learned* policy: a value
head `ψ̂` estimates how much each stored sentence will be needed by *future* sentences, using a
successor-representation-style discounted sum, and the memory evicts the argmin.

🔴 **The primary claim is cognitive, not engineering.** Does a model trained only to predict the next
sentence rediscover Kintsch & van Dijk's 1978 **leading-edge strategy** — the human pattern of
retaining propositions high in a narrative's macrostructure and dropping peripheral detail,
independently of recency? The engineering delta (better perplexity) is **secondary and the spec says
so**. Drifting toward the engineering framing *is* drift, even when every number is honest.

⚠️ **But three of the loop's four supporting numbers do not survive:**

- ⚠️ **"cross-attention KL from uniform 0.0013 nats" is non-diagnostic and directionally backwards.**
  The *untrained* model measures 0.0016–0.0034 and the *trained* model 0.0099–0.0139 — the trained

- ⚠️ **"deleting the entire memory" names an ablation that was not performed.** The ablation sets

## 7. Decisions only Brendan can make — do not make them for him

1. 🔴 **The `b_max` invariant.** `b_max` is FROZEN at 1.0, and it really *is* one SD of `ψ̂`
   (0.98280 ± 0.00360, theory 0.98111 — ✅ verified). **But the argmin turns on the gap between the
   two smallest of `M` draws, not on the spread.** So `b_max` is ~3.2× the median top-2 margin at
   `M = 40` and ~1.9× at `M = 8` (✅ verified independently; swing ~1.66×, and the mechanism is pure
   order statistics, reproduced from first principles). Four options are costed in last night's log.
   **All four need one sentence from Brendan naming the invariant** — equal flip rate? equal
   `b_max`/margin? equal fraction of decisions `b` could cross? *The missing input is the definition
   of "comparable", and no further measurement supplies it.*
   ⚠️ Do not quote "12.8 pp" as the order-statistic effect — that arm also varied `γ_b` and the EMA
   half-life. The isolated effect is ~17 pp.
2. 🔴 **Correction 4 contradicts itself at the `M` D-5 funds.** ✅ Verified: `γ_b = b_max/(0.25·E[lifetime])`
   with `E[lt] = M` gives 0.1 at `M = 40`, 0.25 at `M = 16`, **0.5 at `M = 8`** — 5× over the top of
   its own stated *"order 0.05–0.1"*. The audit's read: this is a **scoping bug**, not a range bug.
   `γ_b` is per-corpus exactly as `M`, `K`, `S` and `A_max` already are, but `constants.py:764` derives
   it with no scope, so the registry will silently hand E7's `M = 8` model a value ~5.7× too small.
3. **Which side of the `ProtectionBias` / `_score` interface moves.** `_score` calls `self.bias.b(slots)`;
   `ProtectionBias` declares `update`/`values`/`reset` and no `b`. A `# type: ignore` is why nothing
   caught it. Evidence favours `ProtectionBias` (§3.5 updates `b_i` from `ū_i` and its own previous
   value — nothing makes it a function of memory state), but **the spec names no accessor**.
4. **§3.4's `− ν · max cos` is unclamped.** ⚠️ The loop's "two sign traps" report is **overstated** —
   both scenarios it named provably cannot change an eviction (one is an argmin over a single
   candidate; the other shifts both members equally, 0 flips in 1000 trials). **But the audit found a
   reachable version the loop missed:** at `n_live ≥ 3`, any slot whose best peer-cosine is *negative*
   gets a protective bonus, and that **does** flip victims. Decide in E1's `ν` sweep whether the term
   should be `− ν · max(0, cos)`.
5. **Per-step vs global normalizer in `_score`** — they differ on 5.79% ± 0.93% of victims. Neither is wrong.
6. **Confirm S-7 was actually sent.** It is the only §16 release-gate edit an unattended loop made
   (`docs/release-conditions.md` condition 4, commit `f1ea1a0`). It *tightens* rather than loosens, and
   nothing in the repo records the authorisation beyond the loop's own prose.

### Settled last night — do not reopen

- ✅ **`ν` cannot arbitrate between two near-copies, at any `ν`.** Verified analytically *and*
  numerically, and the audit calls it **understated**: `score_i(ν)` is affine in `ν` with slope
  `−p_i`, and mutual-nearest-neighbour pair members share `p_i` exactly. Raising `ν` only makes the
  decision land inside such a pair more often (3.1% → 83.1% at `ν = 4`), where it is then a coin flip.
  **So `ν` is not the lever for D-3 and tuning it is wasted effort.** A redundancy fix must be
  *asymmetric* within the pair — leave-one-out marginal value, or a tie-break on age/demand — or
  `max_cos` must be applied as set-level selection rather than a per-slot additive penalty.
- ✅ **`b` and `ν` are not commensurable**, so `ν` cannot be swept once on synthetic and transferred.
  ⚠️ But the loop's stated mechanism is wrong: *neither* term is z-scored, and the conversion factor
  is `SD(max_cos)`, not `SD(ψ̂)` (which cancels). This is a **spec** defect in §3.4, not a code defect
  — do not send anyone to patch `rsr.py`.
- ✅ **`EvictionRecord.attribution` is a config echo**, so §3.4's own decision-attribution test is not
  computable from any run's log. Treat that gate as NOT IMPLEMENTED.
- ✅ **The 03:27 escalation was correctly retracted.** ⚠️ Its stated mechanism is wrong though —
  "α is a function of the pre-query state" is false; α *does* take the query. The load-bearing fact is
  the independence of the planted need from (memory, query). What survives is a theorem, not a
